# Research Ethics & Compliance Guide

## Overview

Conducting ethical research protects research participants, ensures research integrity, and maintains public trust in science. This guide covers key ethical considerations and compliance requirements.

## Part 1: Human Subjects Research

### IRB (Institutional Review Board) Approval

The IRB is responsible for protecting human research subjects.

#### When You Need IRB Approval

**You need IRB approval if your research:**
- Involves human participants
- Collects identifiable data (names, IDs, contact information)
- Involves interviews, surveys, observations, or biospecimens
- Studies vulnerable populations
- Even if research is "minimal risk"

**You likely DON'T need IRB approval if:**
- Research uses only publicly available, already published data
- Research is quality improvement/evaluation for internal use only
- You're analyzing your own teaching/practices (not generalizable research)
- *Always ask your IRB if unsure*

#### IRB Approval Process

**Timeline: 2-6 months before data collection**

1. **Complete CITI Training** (2-3 hours)
   - Go to https://about.citiprogram.org/
   - Register with your institution
   - Select "Responsible Conduct of Research" + discipline-specific courses
   - Download certificate

2. **Develop Research Protocol** (including)
   - Specific research aims and methods
   - Participant characteristics (age, health status, etc.)
   - Recruitment procedures
   - Compensation (if any)
   - Risks and benefits
   - Confidentiality safeguards

3. **Create Informed Consent Form**
   - Plain language explanation of study
   - What participation involves
   - Potential risks and benefits
   - Confidentiality and privacy protections
   - Voluntary participation and right to withdraw
   - Contact information for questions

4. **Submit to IRB**
   - Access institutional IRB system
   - Complete all required forms
   - Attach protocol and consent form
   - List all team members
   - Include CITI certificates

5. **Respond to IRB Questions**
   - IRB may request clarifications
   - Provide detailed responses
   - Revise consent form as needed
   - Submit revisions promptly

6. **Receive IRB Approval Letter**
   - Letter specifies approval date and duration
   - Note expiration date (typically 1 year)
   - Keep approval letter for all records

#### Key IRB Responsibilities During Research

- **Use approved consent form** (no changes without IRB approval)
- **Report any changes** to protocol (even minor)
- **Report any adverse events** immediately
- **Obtain continuing review** annually or at expiration
- **Maintain required records** (consent forms, data)

### Informed Consent

Participants must understand what they're agreeing to.

#### Elements of Informed Consent

1. **Study Purpose**
   - Clear, non-technical explanation
   - Why the research is important

2. **Procedures**
   - What participants will do
   - Time commitment
   - Frequency of participation

3. **Risks**
   - Physical risks
   - Psychological risks
   - Social/economic risks
   - Breach of confidentiality risks

4. **Benefits**
   - Potential direct benefits to participant
   - Potential benefits to society
   - Don't exaggerate benefits

5. **Confidentiality**
   - How data will be protected
   - Who can access data
   - How long data will be kept
   - Secure storage practices

6. **Voluntary Participation**
   - No coercion
   - Right to withdraw at any time
   - No penalties for withdrawal
   - Freedom to decline to answer questions

7. **Contact Information**
   - Researcher contact details
   - IRB office contact for questions about rights

8. **Signatures**
   - Participant signature and date
   - Researcher/witness signature
   - Two copies (one for participant, one for records)

#### Special Considerations

**For Vulnerable Populations:**
- **Children**: Require parent/guardian consent + child assent (age-appropriate)
- **Prisoners**: Extra protections; IRB approval required
- **Cognitively impaired**: Require legally authorized representative + participant assent
- **Pregnant women**: Minimize fetal risk
- **Non-English speakers**: Provide consent in native language

### Confidentiality & Privacy

Protect participant privacy throughout research.

#### De-identification Process

1. **Identify all identifiers**
   - Name, initials
   - Date of birth (use year only if possible)
   - Specific date (use month-year; or season-year)
   - Contact information (address, phone, email)
   - Medical record numbers
   - Health insurance ID
   - Photographs/video recordings
   - Biometrics (fingerprints, voice recordings)
   - Any unique characteristics

2. **Create Code System**
   - Assign unique code/ID to each participant
   - Example: P001, P002, ... P150
   - Store mapping document separately
   - Restricted access to mapping document

3. **Remove Identifiers from Data**
   - Delete or obscure from analysis dataset
   - Keep de-identified data for analysis
   - Keep identified data separate (if needed) for follow-up

**Example**:
```
IDENTIFIED DATA (locked, separate):
ID001: John Smith, DOB: 1985-03-22, Phone: 555-1234

ANALYSIS DATA (shared, published):
P001: Age: 39 (as of study entry), Region: Midwest
```

#### Data Security Practices

- **Encryption**: Full-disk encryption on computers; encrypted external drives
- **Access control**: Password-protected files; user permissions
- **Secure deletion**: Use secure deletion software when removing files
- **Transmission**: Encrypted file transfer (not email for sensitive data)
- **Storage**: Locked filing cabinets for physical records
- **Secure disposal**: Shred paper documents; secure deletion of digital files

#### Data Retention & Destruction

- **Retain**: Typically 3-7 years after study completion
- **Check requirements**: Funding agency; institution; discipline norms
- **Destroy securely**: Shred paper; secure digital deletion
- **Document destruction**: Record when and how data was destroyed

## Part 2: Research Integrity

### Avoiding Research Misconduct

Research misconduct includes fabrication, falsification, and plagiarism.

#### Fabrication (Making Up Data)

**What it is**: Inventing data or results

**Why it happens**: Pressure to publish; disappointing results; deadline pressure

**How to avoid**:
- Preregister studies to prevent selective reporting
- Report null findings and negative results
- Conduct research as planned, not backwards
- Don't "improve" data to match hypothesis
- When in doubt, consult your advisor

**Consequences**: Retraction; loss of funding; career termination; legal action

#### Falsification (Changing Existing Data)

**What it is**: Misrepresenting results; selective reporting; p-hacking

**Examples**:
- Reporting different sample sizes than actually used
- Removing outliers without justification
- Reporting only significant results
- Testing many hypotheses then claiming primary hypothesis

**How to avoid**:
- Pre-specify analysis plan before analysis
- Document any deviations from plan
- Report all analyses, not just significant ones
- Use appropriate statistical controls for multiple tests

**Consequences**: Publication retraction; loss of funding; reputation damage

#### Plagiarism (Using Others' Work Without Attribution)

**What it is**: Presenting others' words or ideas as your own

**Types**:
- Direct copying without quotation marks
- Paraphrasing without citation
- Using ideas/results without attribution
- Self-plagiarism (reusing your own published work without acknowledgment)

**How to avoid**:
- Always cite sources (even when paraphrasing)
- Use quotation marks for direct quotes
- Maintain source records
- Use plagiarism checking tools (Turnitin, Grammarly)
- Get training on proper citation

**Consequences**: Publication rejection; retraction; expulsion; legal action

### Publication Ethics

#### Authorship Decisions

**Discuss before writing**:
- Who deserves authorship (vs. acknowledgment)?
- What is author order?
- Who is corresponding author?

**ICMJE Criteria** (International Committee of Medical Journal Editors):

To be an author, you must:
1. Make substantial contributions to conception or design, OR to acquisition or analysis of data
2. Draft or critically revise intellectual content
3. Approve final version
4. Agree to be accountable for all aspects of work

**Author Order Considerations**:
- First author: Usually did most work
- Middle authors: Contributing authors
- Last author: Often advisor/senior investigator
- Different fields have different conventions

**CRediT Taxonomy** (Contributor Roles Taxonomy):
Document specific contributions (use in author notes):
- Conceptualization
- Data curation
- Formal analysis
- Funding acquisition
- Investigation
- Methodology
- Project administration
- Resources
- Software
- Supervision
- Validation
- Visualization
- Writing – original draft
- Writing – review and editing

#### Conflicts of Interest

**Disclose**:
- Financial interests in research topic
- Consulting relationships
- Stock ownership
- Funding from interested parties

**Management**:
- Public disclosure in manuscript
- Recusal from decision-making (if severe)
- Development of management plan

**Common COIs**:
- Funding from company testing their product
- Consulting fees from industry
- Patent applications related to research
- Job offers related to research findings

#### Data Availability

**Required practices**:
1. Clearly state data availability
2. Specify restrictions (if any)
3. Provide data sharing statement
4. Deposit data in appropriate repository
5. Provide DOI for citation

**Examples**:
- "Data are available on Zenodo: https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.XXXXXX"
- "Data cannot be shared due to participant confidentiality; contact author for data access"
- "Data are openly available in OSF at https://osf.io/XXXXXX"

#### Responsible Reporting

- **Report limitations**: Be honest about study limitations
- **Avoid overstatement**: Stick to what your data shows
- **Put results in context**: How do they fit with existing knowledge?
- **Discuss alternative explanations**: Your data may have alternative interpretations
- **Acknowledge funding**: Disclose financial support

## Part 3: Specific Compliance Requirements

### HIPAA (Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act)

**Applies to**: Research using protected health information (PHI) in U.S.

**What's protected**:
- Names, medical record numbers, social security numbers
- Dates of birth/admission/discharge
- Phone numbers, email addresses
- Financial information
- Biometric data

**Requirements**:
1. Complete HIPAA training
2. Develop privacy and security plans
3. Use Business Associate Agreements if applicable
4. De-identify data (remove 18 identifiers)
5. Maintain audit logs
6. Encrypt data
7. Limit access to authorized personnel

**Penalties**: Up to $1.5 million per violation; potential jail time

### GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation)

**Applies to**: Research with data of EU residents

**Key requirements**:
1. Legal basis for processing (consent, legitimate interest, etc.)
2. Data Protection Impact Assessment
3. Data Protection Officer notification
4. Data subject rights (access, erasure, portability)
5. Breach notification (within 72 hours)
6. Data retention limits

**Penalties**: Up to €20 million or 4% of global revenue

### IRB-Specific Ongoing Compliance

**After Approval**:
- **Continuing review**: Submit annually for approval extension
- **Adverse event reporting**: Report any unexpected harm immediately
- **Protocol modifications**: Get approval before changing procedures
- **Recruitment modifications**: Get approval before changing recruitment
- **Research completion**: File final report with IRB

**Record Keeping**:
- Consent forms (signed by participants)
- IRB approval letters
- Modification approvals
- Adverse event reports
- Study files and documentation
- Retain for typically 3-7 years minimum

## Ethical Decision-Making Framework

When facing ethical questions:

1. **Identify the ethical issue**: What specific concern exists?

2. **Gather information**: What are the facts? Who is affected?

3. **Consider perspectives**: How do different stakeholders view this?

4. **Review guidelines**: What do ethical codes/regulations say?

5. **Consult others**: Talk to advisor, IRB, mentors, ethics committee

6. **Consider alternatives**: What are other options?

7. **Make decision**: Align with ethical principles and regulations

8. **Document reasoning**: Record your decision and why
